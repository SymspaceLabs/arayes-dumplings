Arayes & Blanket Dumplings

One shop, one cook session. Serves 3–4 of each.

Arayes8 halves 4
Dumplings2 lb of filling 40

Both dishes, one run. Roughly 70 minutes. The arayes go in the oven and free your hands for exactly the time the dumplings need. Quantities below follow the scaler above.

Tonight, actual numbers

What's in front of you

  • 11 arayes halves. Skillet: 3 rounds of 4, 4, 3 — medium, 2 min per side, three sides each. Or air fryer: 3 rounds at 360 °F, 10 min, flip at 5. Then all 11 on one sheet pan at 350 °F for 15 min, pull at 155 °F.
  • 21 dumplings across two pans, both on medium.
    • Pan of 8 — 3 tbsp broth, lid on, 10 min
    • Pan of 13 — ¼ cup broth, lid on, 12 min; a crowded pan runs slower
    Check one from each at 160 °F. Lid back on for 2 more if either is short.
  • Bouillon: half a cube dissolved in ½ cup hot water covers both pans. Split it 3 tbsp and 4 tbsp.
  • The steps below still hold — these are just your counts instead of the defaults.

First — 2 minutes

Set up

  1. Oven to 350 °F.
  2. Yogurt into a paper-towel-lined sieve set over a bowl, into the fridge. Strain about a cup to end up with ¾. Skip if you bought Greek.
  3. Two skillets on the stove, a sheet pan on the counter.

Prep — 20 minutes

Knife work, all of it now

  1. Onion. Half a large one, or one small. Rub the cut face on the large holes of a box grater until you reach your knuckles. Squeeze the pulp hard over the sink, bin the liquid. You want about 2–3 tbsp of damp pulp left.
  2. Jalapeño — same grater, no squeezing.
  3. Chop the parsley and cilantro. Slice the green onions.

Mix — 5 minutes

Two bowls

  1. Arayes meat. Mix with your hands until tacky, about a minute. Bowl 1 — arayes
    • 1 lb ground beef
    • The squeezed onion pulp
    • The grated jalapeño
    • 2½ tsp garlic paste
    • 2 tbsp tomato paste
    • ⅓ cup chopped parsley
    • ¼ cup chopped cilantro
    • 1 tbsp olive oil
    • 1 tsp salt
    • 1 tsp black pepper
    • 2 tsp cumin
    • 2 tsp paprika
    • 1 tsp allspice
    • 1 tsp ground coriander
    • 1 tsp garlic powder
    • ¼ tsp cinnamon
  2. Dumpling filling. Mix just until combined — overworked beef turns rubbery. This is the opposite of bowl 1. Bowl 2 — dumplings
    • 1 lb ground beef
    • ⅓ cup sliced green onions
    • 1 tbsp ginger paste
    • 2 tsp garlic paste
    • 1 tbsp oyster sauce
    • 1 tsp black pepper
    • 2 tbsp chili crisp
    • 3 tbsp soy sauce
    • 1½ tsp sesame oil
    • 1 tbsp rice vinegar

Shape — 20 minutes

Stuff and drape

  1. Cut 4 pitas in half. Microwave 10 sec so the pockets open without tearing. Spread a thin layer of meat inside each of the 8 halves — ¼ inch maximum. Brush both outer faces with olive oil.
  2. Scoop 20 mounds of filling onto sheet pans, a rounded tablespoon each, two inches apart. Drape a wrapper over each and pinch down around the base. Damp paper towel over the lot while you work.

Cook — 25 minutes, both pans

Sear, bake, steam

  1. Skillet A — arayes. Medium heat, dial at the halfway mark. Dry pan, no oil (they're already brushed).
    • Cut edge down first — 2 min, until properly charred
    • Flip onto one flat face — 2 min
    • Flip to the other face — 2 min
    Six minutes a round, 4 halves at a time. Two rounds to get through them all. Onto the sheet pan as they finish.
  2. Into the oven — all the halves on one pan, 15 min, until the meat reads 155 °F.
  3. Skillet B — dumplings. Medium heat, halfway on the dial. Run this while the oven does the arayes.
    • Drizzle oil in the cold pan, arrange 10 dumplings
    • Pour ¼ cup broth around them, not over them
    • Lid on — 10 min. Don't lift it before 8, you lose all the steam
    • Check one: 160 °F. Not there? Lid back on, 2 more minutes
    • Optional: once the liquid has cooked off, lid off, medium-high, 2 min for crisp bottoms
  4. Side task while you wait. In a small bowl stir together 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp garlic paste and a pinch of chopped parsley. This gets brushed on the arayes the moment they leave the oven — nothing to do with the dumplings.
  5. Whisk the dipping sauce. Loosen with a spoonful of water at a time if it's too thick to dip. Bowl 3 — tahini yogurt sauce
    • The strained yogurt (¾ cup)
    • ¼ cup tahini
    • 2 tsp garlic paste
    • Juice of ½ lemon
    • 1 tbsp olive oil
    • ½ tsp salt
    • ¼ tsp cumin
    • 2 tbsp parsley + 2 tbsp cilantro
  6. Arayes out at 155 °F. Brush with the garlic-parsley finishing oil while they're still hot — that's when they drink it in. Serve with the tahini yogurt sauce for dipping. This oil is for the arayes only; it never goes near the dumplings.
  7. Dumplings out. Top in this order, straight in the pan:
    • Soy sauce — 1–2 tsp drizzled over the whole pan. Go light, the filling is already salty
    • Chili crisp — a heaped spoonful. Stir the jar first so you get the crunchy bits, not just the oil off the top
    • Green onions — scattered
    • Sesame seeds — a pinch over everything
    No extra oil at this stage. No sesame oil, no olive oil — the chili crisp brings its own. Serve with rice.
  8. Run the second batch — fresh ¼ cup broth each time, wipe the pan if it's gone sticky.

Alternative

Air fryer — arayes only

This replaces the skillet and the oven. Faster, less washing up, and the pita crisps more evenly.

  • 360 °F, 10 minutes, flip at the 5-minute mark.
  • Four halves per go, max. Crowding steams them instead of crisping them.
  • Pull at 155 °F internal, then brush with the finishing oil.
  • No preheat needed for these.

Don't air-fry the dumplings. The wrapper needs wet steam to cook through — dry heat splits it and the dough goes leathery. Those stay in the covered skillet.

The three things that go wrong

  • Wet onion. If it drips, it wasn't squeezed enough, and the pita goes soggy from the inside.
  • Too much meat in the pita. Thicker than ¼ inch and the bread burns before the centre cooks.
  • Overmixed dumpling filling. Stop the second it comes together.

Honest read: these two recipes still barely overlap. Ground beef, garlic, salt and black pepper cross both — everything else is separate. The real savings are within each recipe: garlic and herbs get used two or three times in the arayes, and soy sauce, chili crisp and green onion go in the dumpling filling and on top. Buy for the total, not per recipe.

No pork anywhere: both recipes run on beef now. Two labels worth reading — some chili crisp brands add pork fat or crispy pork bits (Lao Gan Ma and Fly By Jing don't), and a few dumpling wrapper brands use lard instead of oil. Plain flour-and-water wrappers are the safe pick, and every store below sells halal.

    Arayes — meat mixture

    • 1 lb ground beef (85/15)
    • 1 small yellow onion, grated and squeezed dry (half a large one)
    • 5 garlic cloves, finely grated — or 2½ tsp garlic paste
    • 1 jalapeño, grated
    • 2 tbsp tomato paste
    • ⅓ cup parsley, finely chopped
    • ¼ cup cilantro, finely chopped
    • 1 tbsp olive oil
    • 1 tbsp ice water — skip this if you used garlic paste, it carries enough moisture already
    • 1 tsp salt · 1 tsp black pepper
    • 2 tsp cumin · 2 tsp paprika
    • 1 tsp allspice · 1 tsp coriander · 1 tsp garlic powder
    • ¼ tsp cinnamon
    • 4 pitas, cut in half

    Garlic tahini yogurt sauce

    • ¾ cup Greek yogurt — plain whole-milk yogurt or dahi works; strain it 30 min in a paper-towel-lined sieve
    • ¼ cup tahini
    • 3 garlic cloves, finely grated — or 2 tsp garlic paste; paste is milder raw, so go heavier here
    • Juice of ½ lemon
    • 1 tbsp olive oil
    • ½ tsp salt · ¼ tsp cumin
    • 2 tbsp parsley + 2 tbsp cilantro, finely chopped

    To finish

    • Olive oil mixed with minced garlic and parsley

    Method

    1. Heat the oven to 350 °F. Do all the prep below before you touch the meat — once your hands are in it, you won't want to stop.
    2. Grate the onion. Use a small onion whole, or half a large one. Peel it, hold it by the root end and rub the cut face against the large holes of a box grater until you're down near your knuckles, then throw the stub away. It comes out as wet pulp, not shreds — that's right. Now scoop it into your hands over the sink and squeeze hard until it stops dripping. Keep the pulp, bin the juice. You should have roughly 2–3 tbsp left. No grater? Chop it as fine as you can, wrap it in a paper towel and wring it out.
    3. Grate the jalapeño the same way, on the large holes. No need to squeeze this one.
    4. In a large bowl, combine the beef with the squeezed onion, garlic, jalapeño, tomato paste, parsley, cilantro, olive oil and every spice. Mix with your hands until it's fully combined and slightly tacky — about a minute. Using garlic paste? Leave the ice water out.
    5. Cut the pitas in half and gently open each pocket — microwave them a few seconds first so they don't tear. Spread a thin layer of meat inside each half, then brush the outside generously with olive oil.
    6. Heat a pan over medium. Cook meat-side down first for 1–2 minutes until charred, then flip and cook each pita side until golden and crisp.
    7. Move them to a sheet pan and finish in the oven for 15 minutes, or until the meat hits 155 °F in the middle. All 8 halves fit on one pan.
    8. While they're in the oven, whisk the yogurt, tahini, garlic, lemon juice, olive oil, salt, cumin and chopped herbs until smooth. If it's too thick to dip, loosen it with a spoonful of water at a time.
    9. Brush the hot arayes with the garlic-parsley olive oil and serve with the sauce for dipping.

    Things that go wrong

    • Squeeze the onion hard. Wet onion steams the meat and makes the pita soggy from the inside.
    • Thin layer. Thicker than about ¼ inch and the pita burns before the center is cooked.
    • The ice water isn't optional-feeling nonsense — it keeps the mix juicy in a hot pan.
    • 155 °F is the target and it will carry over a few degrees while it rests.

    Blanket dumplings — beef filling

    • 1 lb ground beef (85/15)
    • ⅓ cup green onions, thinly sliced
    • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated — ginger paste swaps 1:1
    • 4 garlic cloves, minced — or 2 tsp garlic paste
    • 1 tbsp oyster sauce
    • 1 tsp black pepper
    • 2 tbsp chili crisp
    • 3 tbsp soy sauce
    • 1½ tsp sesame oil
    • 1 tbsp rice vinegar
    • 20 dumpling wrappers, plus a few spares for tears
    • ¼ cup chicken broth per skillet batch — this one doesn't scale with the recipe
    • Avocado oil

    Topping

    • Soy sauce · chili crisp · green onions · sesame seeds

    Method

    1. In a bowl, combine the beef, green onions, ginger, garlic, oyster sauce, black pepper, chili crisp, soy sauce, sesame oil and rice vinegar. Mix just until it comes together — beef goes rubbery if you work it the way you worked the arayes.
    2. Scoop mounds of filling onto a sheet pan — a rounded tablespoon each, spaced a couple of inches apart so you have room to work. Keep the wrappers under a damp paper towel while you go; they crack within minutes of hitting the air.
    3. Lay a dumpling wrapper over each mound and pinch the edges around the filling to seal — that's the "blanket."
    4. Drizzle with avocado oil, pour in the chicken broth, cover, and steam 10 minutes, or until the beef reaches 160 °F. Move them to a wide skillet for this — a sheet pan can't hold a lid.
    5. Top with soy sauce, chili crisp, green onions and sesame seeds. Serve with rice. Repeat for the remaining batches — fresh broth each time, and wipe the pan if it's gone sticky.

    Things that go wrong

    • Beef instead of pork. It sets firmer and tastes leaner, so don't overmix it — stop as soon as it's combined, or you get a bouncy meatball texture. If your beef is leaner than 85/15, add a tablespoon of ice water to the filling.
    • The pan, not the sheet pan. Step 4 needs a lid and a stovetop burner — use a wide skillet. A sheet pan is fine for scooping and shaping, then transfer.
    • Keep wrappers under a damp towel. They dry out and crack in about two minutes of air.
    • Wet your fingertip and run it along the wrapper edge before pinching — it seals far better.
    • Don't crowd the pan. They swell, stick together, and tear when you lift them.
    • You can freeze them raw on the sheet pan, then bag them. Steam from frozen, add 3–4 minutes.

    Both in one night: roughly 70 minutes start to table. The arayes go in the oven and free up your hands for exactly the ten minutes the dumplings need.

    Prep once, not twice

    • Garlic paste instead of cloves? 1 tsp paste = 2 cloves. At your scale: 2½ tsp in the arayes meat, 2 tsp in the sauce, 1 tsp in the finishing oil, 4 tsp in the dumpling filling. About 3 tbsp all in. Ginger paste swaps 1:1.
    • Garlic, fresh: peel them all at once. Grate for the arayes, mince for the dumplings and the finishing oil.
    • Herbs: chop the whole parsley bunch and the whole cilantro bunch in one go, then portion. Parsley gets split three ways, cilantro two.
    • Green onions: slice the whole bunch — ⅓ cup goes in the filling, the rest is topping.
    • Box grater, large holes handles the onion and jalapeño. Squeeze the onion pulp dry over the sink and throw the liquid away — that step is what keeps the pita from going soggy.
    • Yogurt: if you're using regular yogurt or dahi rather than Greek, set it to strain in a paper-towel-lined sieve the moment you start. Thirty minutes and it's Greek-thick.

    Or split them across two nights

    • The arayes meat mixture keeps 2 days covered in the fridge and honestly improves overnight.
    • Assembled dumplings freeze raw on a sheet pan, then go in a bag — they'll keep a month and cook straight from frozen.
    • The tahini yogurt sauce keeps about 4 days. It thickens; loosen it with water.
    • Both recipes need a pan, a lid and an oven. Doing them on separate nights means one skillet, not two.
